Can sedation help with dental anxiety?

Tell us whether a previous visit, needles, fear of pain or a longer appointment makes care difficult. Also tell us about a strong gag reflex or dental care you have delayed because of anxiety.

Dr. Wagner considers those concerns alongside your health and planned treatment when discussing dental care options.

What is IV sedation?

IV sedation delivers sedative medicine through a vein. The route does not determine how deeply you will be sedated. Dr. Wagner can explain the intended level and what it means for your visit.

Possible benefits

Less anxiety during dental care

IV sedation may reduce anxiety during treatment. Awareness and response vary with the plan and the individual.

Because local anesthesia handles numbing separately, discuss both anxiety control and pain control before treatment.

Risks and limitations

Being asleep is not guaranteed

“Sleep dentistry” is an everyday phrase, not a promise of unconsciousness or no memory of the visit.

Possible effects include drowsiness and nausea. Your health, medicines and intended sedation level affect planning, monitoring and recovery.

How other sedation terms differ

Wagner Sleep Dental offers IV sedation. Oral sedation and nitrous oxide are explained for comparison, not as additional services offered here.

Oral sedation

What patients say about their sedation visits

Common questions about IV sedation.

What if needles are part of my anxiety?

IV sedation requires access to a vein. Tell the team whether the needle, the sensation or seeing the equipment concerns you so it can be discussed before you decide.

Is IV sedation the same as general anesthesia?

No. General anesthesia is a state of unconsciousness. IV sedation describes medicine delivered through a vein and does not by itself define the depth of sedation. Dr. Wagner can explain the intended level for your care.

How should I plan work or caring responsibilities afterward?

Arrange the required ride or support and follow your discharge instructions. Ask the team when you may safely return to driving, work or caring responsibilities.

Is “sleep dentistry” treatment for sleep apnea?

No. Here the phrase refers to sedation during dental care. Sleep apnea assessment and treatment are separate from dental sedation. Ask the appropriate clinician about sleep-related breathing concerns.

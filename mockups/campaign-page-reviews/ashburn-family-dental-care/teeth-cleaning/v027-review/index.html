What does a dental cleaning remove?

Professional dental cleaning complements brushing and flossing at home.

Plaque
A sticky film that daily brushing and flossing help remove.
Tartar
Hardened plaque that needs professional removal.
Surface stains
Polishing can reduce some staining; it is not teeth whitening.

Regular visits support prevention and early attention to oral-health concerns. Read the CDC’s oral-health prevention advice.

Care for every stage of life

Care should reflect your needs, whether you are a busy professional, arranging family visits, preserving gum health as you get older, or managing sensitivity or a history of gum disease.

Preventive visits, advice on home care and early attention to concerns are part of our general dentistry services.

Routine cleaning versus periodontal treatment.

Routine cleaning and treatment for gum disease serve different needs. Your examination determines which procedure is appropriate.

Routine preventive cleaning

Preventive care to help maintain healthy teeth and gums when your examination supports routine cleaning.

Polishing can reduce surface stains; professional teeth whitening is a separate treatment.

Periodontal treatment

When gum disease needs treatment below the gumline, the dentist may recommend scaling and root planing rather than a routine cleaning. This is treatment for a diagnosed condition, not a more intensive version of routine preventive care.

Periodontal maintenance

After periodontal treatment, ongoing maintenance supports gum care. The interval between these visits follows your findings and care plan.

Dental cleaning experiences in our Ashburn location.

What happens at a teeth-cleaning visit?

Your visit includes a review of your concerns and an assessment of your teeth and gums.

Discuss your concerns

Share your last cleaning, health or medicine changes, and any bleeding, tenderness, buildup or sensitivity. An exam usually accompanies cleaning to look for early concerns. Dental X-rays are recommended when needed, not automatically at every visit.

Cleaning and polishing

The team removes buildup using appropriate techniques and tools, with polishing when suitable.

Sensitive teeth or a long gap since your last visit?

Tell us about sensitivity before cleaning begins so techniques and tools can be adjusted. If you are overdue, share when you were last seen and any changes you have noticed.

Bring your medication list, relevant medical history and insurance information if applicable. Patient forms are available before your appointment.

Questions before your visit.

How long does a dental cleaning take?

Most cleaning appointments take 45 minutes to an hour. The amount of buildup, additional evaluation and any treatment needed can change the time. Confirm the time to allow when arranging your visit.

Can teeth cleaning help improve bad breath?

Yes. Professional cleaning removes plaque, tartar and bacteria that can contribute to bad breath, supporting a fresher, cleaner mouth. Mention persistent bad breath during your assessment.

Is teeth cleaning safe for sensitive teeth?

Yes. Tell the team before cleaning begins so techniques and tools can be adjusted for sensitivity.

Will a cleaning remove coffee or tobacco stains?

Cleaning can remove many surface stains from coffee, tea or tobacco. Deeper stains may need a different approach, such as cosmetic whitening.

Do I need a dental exam with every cleaning?

An exam often accompanies cleaning. Confirm which services are planned and whether the exam or imaging is charged separately.

What if it has been years since my last cleaning?

Request an assessment to find out which care is appropriate. You do not need to choose a cleaning procedure yourself.

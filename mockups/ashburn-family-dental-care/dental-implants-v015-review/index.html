UNDERSTANDING YOUR OPTIONS

What are dental implants?

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth. This small titanium post sits in the jawbone and supports a replacement crown, bridge or denture.

As the bone heals around the implant over several months, it anchors the replacement tooth. A connector called an abutment joins the implant to the crown, bridge or denture above it.

At our Ashburn office, we check your gums, jawbone and health before recommending implants. If your jaw needs more support, we may recommend a bone graft first. Your examination helps determine which replacement and preparation are right for you.

Why consider implants?

  • Independent support. No neighboring teeth used as anchors.
  • Steadier dentures. Implants can reduce movement.
  • Natural look and feel. Designed for everyday chewing.
  • Long-term potential. Can last for years with proper care.

Are implants right for you?

Your dentist checks your gums, jawbone and health before recommending implants. Discuss smoking, medications and health conditions.

Benefits, healing and longevity vary. Daily cleaning and dental checkups still matter.

ONE TOOTH OR SEVERAL

Which replacement suits you?

The number and position of missing teeth help shape the discussion. Your dentist will compare suitable options after examining your mouth.

One missing tooth

A single-tooth implant supports a crown to fill one gap without using neighboring teeth as supports. The surrounding teeth and bone are part of the assessment.

Several missing teeth

An implant-supported bridge is anchored to implants; a conventional bridge uses neighboring teeth for support. Compare dental bridge options with your dentist based on the gap, bone and supporting teeth.

Most teeth in an arch

Implant dentures replace multiple teeth and attach to implants for stability. Fixed and removable designs differ in how you clean them; suitability and the number of implants depend on your individual plan.

FROM EVALUATION TO FOLLOW-UP

Implant treatment at Ashburn Family Dental Care.

At your consultation, the dentist evaluates your missing teeth and discusses whether implants or another replacement may suit you.

You can compare options without committing to treatment. The plan determines preparation, healing and surgery dates. Implant placement and the final tooth replacement are separate stages.

  1. Evaluation and planning

    Discuss your goals, symptoms, current restorations and possible alternatives. Bring your medical history, medication list and insurance information if you have it. The dentist may recommend imaging to assess the missing-tooth site. Review the plan and estimate before deciding.

  2. Preparation and placement

    Preparation may include a bone graft if the jaw needs support. The implant post is placed as part of the treatment sequence agreed with your dentist.

  3. Healing and restoration

    The implant needs time to join with the jawbone. After healing, we bring you back to design and place the crown, bridge or denture. Timing varies with the site and treatment needed.

  4. Follow-up and long-term care

    Follow your dentist’s instructions for eating, cleaning and recovery. Keep follow-up appointments and ongoing dental care to monitor the gums, implants and replacement teeth.

Questions about
implant treatment.

Will I have teeth while the implant heals?

A temporary tooth replacement may be possible while the implant heals or the final tooth is being made. The dentist needs to decide what can be worn safely at your implant site; this is planned individually.

How long does implant treatment take?

Healing between stages often takes several months. Bone grafting or other preparation can lengthen the overall process. Your dentist sets the sequence and restoration timing after evaluating the site and checking healing.

What should I expect after implant placement?

Some soreness, swelling, bruising or minor bleeding can occur after surgery. Follow your clinician’s eating, cleaning and recovery instructions. Contact the treating dentist if pain or swelling worsens, or if the implant feels loose.

What is the difference between fixed and removable implant teeth?

Fixed replacements stay attached; only a dental professional removes them. Removable implant dentures are designed for you to take out for cleaning. Both need daily care around the implants, with a design and cleaning routine chosen for your mouth.

How do I care for dental implants?

Brush and clean between the replacement teeth as your dentist shows you, including around the implant and gumline. Keep regular professional cleanings and implant checks. The cleaning tools and follow-up interval depend on the restoration and your gum health.

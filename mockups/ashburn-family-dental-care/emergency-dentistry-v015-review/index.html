When to seek medical help

Call 911 or go to an emergency department for trouble breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, a serious facial or jaw injury, or swelling spreading toward the eye or neck. These symptoms need urgent medical help.

If the office cannot be reached, do not wait for a reply when those warning signs are present.

Pain or infection

Severe tooth pain, swelling or a suspected abscess needs prompt dental assessment. Tell the team about any tooth decay and pain, and whether you also have fever, a bad taste or increasing swelling. Treatment for infection inside a tooth may include root canal treatment, depending on the examination.

Injury or damage

Knocked-out or loose permanent teeth, or lost, cracked or broken teeth need dental assessment. For a loose, broken or lost filling or crown, mention pain or sensitivity when you call. Repair or replacement of a dental crown depends on the tooth's condition.

Gum concerns

Inflamed or bleeding gums. Explain how much bleeding there is and whether you also have pain or swelling.

Your visit

What happens at an emergency visit?

At an emergency visit, the dentist evaluates the source of pain, explains the findings and discusses treatment options. Our goal is care suited to your needs.

  1. Assessment

    Describe the problem. The dentist examines the area and may recommend imaging.

  2. Options and estimate

    Discuss the findings, appropriate treatment choices and expected charges before deciding.

  3. Care and next steps

    Treatment, stabilization or referral may be appropriate. Definitive care and follow-up can require another visit.

Dental sedation is available. Discuss its suitability for your planned care with the dentist.

Questions before you call.

What should I do if the office is closed?

For an after-hours dental emergency, call our office and follow the recorded instructions for the emergency line. If you cannot reach help, contact another urgent dental service. Follow the medical warning above for symptoms that need hospital-level care. Do not wait for a website-form reply if you need urgent help.

What should I do if an adult tooth is knocked out?

Seek dental assessment right away. Handle an adult tooth by the crown, not the root, and keep it moist in a small container of milk while arranging care. Do not replant a baby tooth. Read the ADA's emergency guidance for more information.

MedlinePlus: broken or knocked-out teeth

Can I come in today or walk in?

Same-day appointments depend on the day's schedule. Call before coming so reception can coordinate your arrival. Walk-ins are added to the schedule and may involve a wait. Treatment is decided after the examination and can require another visit.

Can I call if I am a new patient?

Yes. New patients can call our office about a dental emergency. Describe your symptoms so reception can coordinate your visit, and see what to bring before you arrive.

What if the tooth cannot be saved?

A tooth that cannot be restored may need to be removed. After the examination, ask about the options, whether referral is needed, and whether later tooth replacement would be appropriate.

When to seek medical help

For trouble breathing or swallowing, heavy bleeding that will not stop, or a serious facial injury, call 911 or go to an emergency department.

Tooth pain, swelling or an abscess

Severe tooth pain, swelling or a suspected dental abscess needs prompt dental assessment. Tell the team where it hurts and when it started. An abscess is an infection in a tooth or the gums; it needs dental treatment.

A broken, loose or knocked-out tooth

A chipped, cracked, broken, loose or knocked-out adult tooth needs dental attention. Explain how the injury happened and whether there is bleeding.

A lost filling or crown

Tell the team if a filling or crown is loose, broken or missing, and whether the tooth is painful or sensitive.

Emergency care, in our patients’ words

Your visit

What happens at an emergency visit?

At an emergency visit, the dentist examines the problem and explains the treatment options.

Assessment

Tell us where it hurts, when it started and about any swelling or injury. At the visit, the dentist examines the area and takes any necessary X-rays, even if another dentist has already assessed the tooth.

Options and estimate

The dentist explains the findings, treatment options and expected charges before you decide. The emergency examination and X-rays are separate from the cost of treatment.

Care and next steps

Treatment may begin at this visit when appropriate and when the dentist’s schedule allows. Otherwise, care may involve stabilization, a follow-up visit or specialist referral. The goals are to address discomfort and limit further damage; the team explains aftercare and next steps.

Bring photo ID and insurance information, if applicable. New patients: confirm your arrival time when booking so there is time for paperwork.

Questions before your visit

Can I book an emergency visit as a new patient?

Yes. Call our office to arrange a problem-focused assessment. Tell the team what happened and whether you have pain or swelling so they can explain the next step before you come in.

Do I need an oral surgeon or a root canal specialist?

We are a general dental practice. We provide some root canals and simple extractions; surgical or more complex cases may need an oral surgeon or endodontist, a root canal specialist. The dentist’s assessment determines whether treatment here or referral is appropriate.

Will someone answer if I call after hours?

You may reach a recording rather than a team member. Follow the after-hours instructions. Do not wait for a dental callback if you have the medical warning signs listed above.

What can I do about a toothache before my visit?

Call and describe the pain and when it started. You can rinse gently with warm water. For swelling after an injury, apply a cold compress outside the cheek. Use medicines only as directed on the label or by a pharmacist, and never place aspirin on the tooth or gums.

What if an adult tooth is knocked out?

Seek urgent dental assessment. Hold the tooth by the crown, not the root, and keep it moist in milk while arranging care. Acting promptly may improve the chance of saving a permanent tooth, but it cannot be guaranteed. Do not put a baby tooth back into its socket.

What if a crown or filling falls out?

Keep the restoration if you have it, avoid chewing on that tooth and contact the office. The dentist needs to assess the tooth before repair or replacement. Do not use household glue or attempt a permanent repair yourself.

How can I reduce the risk of a dental emergency?

Keep up with brushing, flossing and routine dental visits. Wear a mouthguard during sports and avoid chewing hard items that can damage teeth. These habits reduce risk but cannot prevent every emergency.

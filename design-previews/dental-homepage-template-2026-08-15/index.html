Homepage optimization implementation guide

Before building the dental homepage

Complete the research and architecture work first. This implementation guide is documentation for the team and must never be published as patient-facing website content.

Information required before writing

  • Exact practice name, primary location, address, phone, hours, booking method and primary conversion.
  • Dentist names, credentials, treatment responsibilities and verified first-party portraits.
  • Core service hubs, priority treatments, recent indexable articles, new-patient process, emergency route, insurance and financing facts.
  • Original media, attributable reviews, technology, cases, associations and authoritative credential sources.
  • Top three relevant secondary locations, internal destination URLs, metadata, schema and current homepage content.

Research and source order

  • Start with confirmed information supplied by the practice or project owner.
  • Then inspect the website CMS, page-builder content, media, menus, metadata, schema and rendered output when access is available.
  • Confirm public facts through official business listings, official profiles, licensing bodies, universities, hospitals, associations or manufacturers.
  • Record each claim, source, confidence, conflict, allowed wording and missing information in a project research brief.
  • Never store passwords or credentials in the research brief.

Whole-page construction rules

  • Order the page from identity and patient needs to services, providers, evidence, visit information, recent guidance and action.
  • Give every section one patient purpose and one useful relationship to the practice.
  • Use homepage-level summaries; move treatment depth, risks, recovery and candidacy to service pages.
  • Preserve strong existing copy, facts and first-party media. Merge duplication and remove unsupported filler.
  • Use visible review placeholders for unresolved facts. Never invent reviews, awards, statistics, credentials or services.

Final quality gates

  • Public copy must sound human and practice-specific without SEO or workflow terminology.
  • Internal links belong naturally inside useful sentences and never in headings.
  • External links must be sparse, authoritative and directly support a nearby credential or factual claim.
  • Inspect desktop and mobile for hero readability, clipping, overlap, missing media and awkward transitions.
  • Run an independent text-only review before implementation; resolve every rewrite, merge, move, remove or missing finding.
OVERALL HOMEPAGE PROMPT
Audit and prepare a dental-practice homepage using confirmed practice information, available website or CMS content, rendered-page evidence, official public business information, official dentist sources, first-party media, and the approved homepage structure.

Before drafting, create: (1) a Keep/Rewrite/Merge/Move/Remove/Missing section audit, (2) a sourced homepage entity brief, and (3) an internal-link, external-source, doctor-media, and location-route map. Resolve conflicts or mark them for confirmation. Do not invent facts.

Write natural patient-facing copy that clearly connects the practice, primary location, dentists, patient needs, core services, evidence, visit process, and appointment action. Keep treatment depth on service pages. Preserve useful existing wording. Remove duplicated, generic, robotic, keyword-led, or unsupported copy. Build a review-only HTML mockup, visually inspect desktop and mobile, then run an independent text-only editorial and semantic review.

Verified facts and trust elements

Three to five items

What it must do

Give patients three to five quickly scannable, supportable reasons to understand the practice. Each item must contain one fact or one close fact-and-meaning pair.

Information needed

  • Verified training, experience, practice history or access attributes.
  • Source and confidence for every item.
  • The patient-relevant meaning of a credential, when supported.

Construction limits

  • Heading: two to six words.
  • Support: one 12–20-word sentence; never more than 24 words.
  • Move dates, lists and detailed explanations to the dentist or evidence section.

Do / do not

  • Do translate professional terms into factual plain language.
  • Do keep each item understandable without its icon.
  • Do not imply guaranteed quality, safety, eligibility or outcomes.
  • Do not use generic claims such as “best care” or “state-of-the-art” without evidence.

Review only the extracted visible homepage text, final section order, verified entity brief, homepage template guidance, and link map. You did not write the draft.
